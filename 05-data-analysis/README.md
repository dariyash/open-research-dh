# Module 5 · Data Analysis & Documentation

## Aim
Develop a structured, project-specific reproducible workflow for **preparing, analysing, visualising, and
documenting** heritage data, and interpret the results honestly.

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Develop a structured, project-specific reproducible research workflow.
- Apply that workflow to select, clean, and transform heritage data, recording the important decisions.
- Create basic analyses and visualisations appropriate to a research question, and interpret them in
 relation to the strengths and limitations of the dataset.

## Theory, the cycle of DH research, and what gets documented
A typical cycle: **obtain > clean/transform > analyse > visualise > interpret > document > share.** You
have already obtained (Module 3) and filtered (Module 4) your data. Here you close the loop to an
interpretation.

- **Do you need to clean?** Sometimes. Cleaning means *small, honest* tidying, consistent text, removing blank groups, standardising a date field, done *before* counting. It never means inventing or altering values. Always record what you did.
- **Make variables** that answer your question, e.g. counts per institution, per country, per decade.
- **Visualise simply.** A clear, labelled bar chart that shows the data well beats a complicated one with too much information.
- **Write a narrative.** Describe the distribution (where there is more, where less), note
 things that stands out, and state a limitation. Avoid drawing conclusions the data cannot support.

## Hands-on tutorial
**Notebook: [`03_data_analysis.ipynb`](03_data_analysis.ipynb)**, load the filtered dataset, count
records per institution (or country, type, year, you choose), make a bar chart, and write a short
narrative with limitations.

## Task
Using your obtained-and-filtered dataset, transform it to answer your research question, produce at least
one visualisation, and write a 3-4 sentence interpretation: the pattern you see, how it relates to your
question, and one limitation (missing data, small sample, collection bias, name-variant coverage).

## Self-check
- What is the difference between *cleaning* data and *changing* data?
- Why keep interpretations clear and tied to the evidence?
- Name one limitation of your dataset and how you would state it to a reader.
