## Aim
Establish the principles of open research and the humanities-specific context in which they apply.

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this module you will be able to:
- Explain the main principles of open research in the humanities, **open data, open tools, open
 methods, FAIR, CARE, transparency, and reproducibility**, and why they matter for historical and
 heritage research.

## What openness means here
"Open research" is an umbrella for practices that make scholarship **transparent, traceable,
verifiable, reusable, and collaborative**. It grew out of *Open Science*, but the humanities need
their own framing, *Open Humanities*, because humanities research is often interpretive, works with
heterogeneous and culturally sensitive materials, and depends on context that a bare dataset cannot
carry.

Key principles you will meet throughout the course:

- **Open data**: sharing the datasets behind the argument, with clear licences.
- **Open tools & methods**: using software and workflows others can inspect and re-run.
- **Transparency**: showing *how* a result was produced, not just the final result.
- **Reproducibility**: enabling another person to follow your steps and reach the same outcome.
- **FAIR**: data that are *Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable* (Module 8).
- **CARE**: a complementary ethics lens for heritage data: *Collective benefit, Authority to control,
 Responsibility, Ethics*, because "open" is not neutral and touches rights and communities.

FAIR and CARE are treated together in this course: technical openness (FAIR) and responsible,
rights-aware openness (CARE) belong side by side.

## Guided task
Watch both webinars and note, in a few sentences each:
1. Which principle of openness is most relevant to *your* field or project, and why.
2. One tension Andrea Wallace raises between "open" and rights/ethics, and how it might apply to a
 dataset you care about.

## Self-check
- Can you define open data, open methods, FAIR, and CARE in one sentence each?
- Can you give one reason openness in the humanities differs from the natural sciences?
- Can you name one risk of treating "open" as automatically good?
