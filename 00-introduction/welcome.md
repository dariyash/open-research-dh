# Open Research in the Digital Humanities

Welcome to **Open Research in the Digital Humanities**, a self-paced course from the
Humanities Data Science and Methodology (HDSM) group and DisLab, supported by the HERMES project. This course is about making digital humanities research more transparent, responsible, understandable, accessible, and open for reuse.

Module 1 introduces Open Humanities and helps to distinguish this field from broader approaches to open science and research.
Module 2 focuses on documentation as a systemic practice.
Module 3 explores open cultural-heritage data that can be explored to address various research questions.
Module 4 examines how to document the use of artificial intelligence in research.
Module 5 takes you through data preparation, analysis, visualisations, and documenting the cycle of Digital Humanities research.
Module 6 is optional. It introduces data enhancement through external data sources such as Wikidata.
Module 7 focuses on reproducibility. You will audit your workflow using dedicated tools and improve your documentation accordingly.
Module 8 prepares you for responsible publishing, including what you can publish openly and whether there are any restrictions.
Together, the modules guide you through the main stages of open Digital Humanities research. We recommend following them in order, but you can also use individual modules when you need support at a particular stage of your project.

Each module combines short introductons to the module, theoretical input in videos, followed by hands-on tutorials. Learners will practice open research guided by clear instructions, using prepared Jupyter Notebooks and sample datasets, completing self-reflection tasks and checklists, and exploring recommended reading for further learning.
This course is based on the concept of learning by doing. During the course, beginners can work with prepared datasets and the code included in the course. More experienced learners can follow the course path using their own datasets.
